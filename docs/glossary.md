# Glossary

Every key term from the seven units, A–Z. Each entry links to the unit where it is explained in full; open the unit's **Key terms** section or use the search box for the detailed section.

!!! tip "How to use this page"
    Cover the definitions and test yourself. Exam answers should *start* with a definition like these, then explain and give an example. This page is generated from the units' Key terms lists (`planning/scripts/build_glossary.py`), so it always matches them.

## A

Address space
:   The collection of all addresses in a network. Each terminal has a unique address so messages are routed to the correct recipient. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Agent-based model (ABM)
:   A simulation of many individual agents and their interactions. *(Definition beyond the notes.)* — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Agile
:   A flexible, evolutionary methodology using self-organised, cross-functional teams and short iterations (sprints) reviewed with the customer. It "tames the change". — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

ATM
:   Automated Teller Machine: retail banking technology for balance enquiry, interest and exchange rates, money transfer, deposits and withdrawals. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Authoritarian vs democratic technology (Mumford)
:   System-centred, immensely powerful but unstable / man-centred, relatively weak but resourceful and durable. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

## B

Bandwidth divide
:   The continuing gap in connection quality that persists even when everyone has access. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Big data (two-fold core)
:   (1) New data sources: digital footprints used as low-cost, real-time proxy indicators; (2) data analytics placed at the forefront of intelligent decision-making, whatever the scale. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

## C

COBIT / ITIL / DAMA-DMBOK / CMMI
:   Frameworks for ICT governance and management / IT service lifecycle / data management / software development maturity. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Colonialism
:   The establishment, exploitation, maintenance, acquisition and expansion of a colony in one territory by a political power from another territory. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

Colonisation
:   (Latin *colere*, "to inhabit") an ongoing process of control by which a central power dominates surrounding lands and their "resources" through establishing a colony. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

Commoditization
:   The process by which components become interchangeable and compete mainly on price. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

Compulsory licensing
:   A government requiring an IP owner to allow others to use the invention. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

Concierge testing
:   Getting feedback on made-up products from potential customers in everyday settings. It sparks creativity (unlike WOZ, which tests theories). — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

Convergence
:   The double process by which information artifacts and social worlds are fitted into each other. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

## D

Data friction
:   The imperfect operability of IIs at interfaces, where data can be garbled, misinterpreted or lost. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Data fusion
:   Combining redundant data from different sources to make up for the messiness and incompleteness of big data. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Design methodology
:   A system or model that gives structure or consistency to focus on problems and drive success within a design process, and aligns stakeholders. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

Design–reality gap
:   The difference between the requirements or assumptions built into a project's design and the current reality on the ground. — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

Design–reality gap model / framework
:   An ICT4D framework that measures this gap on seven ITPOSMO dimensions, each on a 0–10 scale, to assess project status and explain success or failure of IT projects in low-income countries. — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

Design Sprint
:   A week-long framework: find, sketch, decide, prototype, test. Without testing it is not a design sprint. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

Development informatics
:   The study of the role of ICTs for sustainable socio-economic development, including their contribution to public services and the public sector. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Diffusion of innovations (Rogers)
:   Adoption depends on relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability and observability. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Digital age
:   The era in which individuals en masse move from face-to-face interactions to interactions in digital space. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

Digital archiving
:   Paperless storage of documents and data with DMS and archiving software. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Digital divide
:   The discrepancy in access to ICT between richer and poorer countries and individuals. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

Digital footprint
:   The data trail produced, almost inevitably and as a by-product, by every digital communication and transaction. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Digital health
:   An umbrella term covering e-Health (including m-Health) plus big data, genomics and AI. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Divergence / convergence
:   Generating as many ideas as possible (brainstorming) / reviewing them to produce the most viable design. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

## E

e-Government
:   Using ICT, especially web-based Internet applications, to give citizens, businesses and employees convenient access to government information and services, improve service quality and enhance democratic participation. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

e-Health
:   (WHO) the use of ICT in support of health and health-related fields. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

EHR
:   Electronic health record, a fundamental component of e-Health. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Enactment / organizing / institutionalization
:   The three spheres of scaling IIs. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

End of theory
:   Replacing theory with plain pattern detection by data mining and machine learning. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Engineering design process (EDP)
:   A multi-step process (plan, test, improve) that distinguishes engineering from tinkering. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Engineering mindset
:   The values, attitudes and thinking skills associated with engineering ("engineering habits of mind"). — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

e-Science
:   Combining empirical big data with computer modelling, especially agent-based models. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Extreme Programming (XP)
:   A 2000s collaborative software development method that boosted co-design. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

## F

Facilitator
:   The person in charge of a PD session's structure: atmosphere, time, redirection, encouraging ideas, reading non-verbal language. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

Fourth Industrial Revolution
:   The ICT-driven industrial revolution, currently in its infancy, expected to bring the smart factory and an interoperable digital ecosystem of machines and partners. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

Free riding
:   Copying another's innovation without bearing the R&D cost. IP law prevents it. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

## G

G2C / G2B / G2G / G2E
:   Government-to-Citizen / -Business / -Government / -Employee. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Gadget / ICT gadget
:   A gadget is a tool or machine that helps to do something. An ICT gadget is information and communication equipment, e.g. a printer, radio, TV, ATM, fax machine or mobile phone. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Gap score
:   A 0–10 rating per dimension: 0 = no change (no gap), 5 = some change, 10 = complete change (radical gap). — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

GIS
:   A computer system for capturing, storing, checking and displaying data related to positions on Earth's surface. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Groupware
:   Software that helps a group work together on a shared task. *(Beyond the notes.)* — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

GSM
:   Global System for Mobile communication. It allows roaming. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

## H

HDI
:   Human Development Index: a coarse barometer built on life expectancy, adult literacy, school enrolment ratio and GDP per capita. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Health informatics tools
:   Formal medical terms, clinical guidelines, computers, and information and communication systems. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Human capacity building
:   Investing in people (education, digital literacy, ICT skills) so they can harness ICT. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

## I

ICT
:   A diverse set of technological tools and resources used to transmit, store, create, share or exchange information (UIS, 2009). Generally: all devices, networking components and applications that help people and organisations interact in the digital world. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

ICT4D
:   ICT for Development. — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

Informatics
:   A diverse field of study focused on the transformation of information, specifically how it is processed and communicated. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Information artifact
:   Tools, systems, interfaces and devices for storing, tracking, displaying and retrieving information. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Information infrastructure (II)
:   A large, evolving, embedded, taken-for-granted network of systems, standards and practices that carries information across space and time. Built on an installed base; visible upon breakdown. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Information poverty
:   Lack of access to relevant information needed for decisions that improve wellbeing. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Infrastructuring
:   The ongoing collective practice of creating, implementing, using, maintaining and repairing infrastructures, managing their tensions. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Inherently political technologies
:   Man-made systems that appear to require, or be strongly compatible with, particular political relationships. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Instant messaging ("chatting")
:   Communicating live with someone through text, pictures and even video, received immediately. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Institutionalised
:   Built into the organisation's normal, permanent way of working. — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

Interoperability
:   The ability of different ICT products and systems to share and mutually use data. It is promoted by technology standards. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

ISO/IEC 38500
:   The starting-point standard for ICT governance: six high-level principles for good corporate governance of IT, focused on the board. It does not cover specific processes. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

ISSA Guidelines on ICT
:   Guidance for social security institutions on systematic ICT governance and on applying relevant standards. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

IT (Information Technology)
:   Managing the technologies related to information (software, hardware, networking). Excludes telecommunications. A subset of ICT. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

ITPOSMO
:   Information, Technology, Processes, Objectives and values, Staffing and skills, Management systems and structures, Other resources (time and money). — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

## K

Knowledge Aggregation Platform (KAP)
:   The prototype ICT intervention iterated with users in the creative design stage. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Knowledge management
:   The defining feature of ICTs: helping people collect, manage, store, retrieve and distribute knowledge. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

## L

Lean Startup
:   A methodology that prioritises essential features and builds the most basic version first (the MVP). — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

Leapfrogging
:   (Schumpeterian) using transformational ICTs to skip stages of development and close technology gaps. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Longitudinal study / cohort
:   Repeated observation of the same items over a long period / a group sharing a characteristic or experience in a defined period. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Lucas critique / Goodhart's law / Campbell's law
:   The same 1976 argument in economics, finance and education: any change in policy systematically alters the structure of models built on past data. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

## M

Metadata
:   "Data of data": classes of data describing files and objects. "Almost standards". — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

m-Health
:   The use of mobile wireless technologies for health. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Middleware
:   Software that sits between applications and the operating system or network and connects them (one of the ten ICT components). — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

Mid-implementation tool
:   Using DRG analysis on a running project to steer it, as in the e-government case. — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

MVP
:   Minimum Viable Product: the most basic version containing the essential ("most valuable") features. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

## N

Naive technological determinism
:   The idea that technology develops by its own internal dynamic and, unmediated, moulds society. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Nowcasting
:   Updating estimates of the present in real time, the best that data analytics can do because all data is from the past. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

## O

OASIS, W3C, OMG, Dublin Core
:   Bodies setting technical standards for interoperability, metadata, semantic and web technologies. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Outside world
:   The eighth row in Figure 3.2: the project's external environment. Not part of the 7-dimension score. — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

## P

Participatory design (PD)
:   A collaborative, democratic methodology in which real-world users and stakeholders take part in design with professional designers. Also called cooperative design, community design, co-design or co-creation. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

PBX / VoIP
:   Private Branch Exchange (an organisation's private telephone switch) / Voice over Internet Protocol (voice carried as data over IP networks). The two ways teleconferencing transmits data. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Persuasive technology
:   Any interactive computing system designed to change attitudes or behaviour. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Pre-hoc (predictive) analysis
:   DRG analysis done before implementation. Table 3.1 was designed for this. — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

Project champion
:   A committed person who drives a project forward. Champions were one of the small-gap strengths in the e-government case. — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

Proxy indicator
:   An easily measured variable that correlates with the variable of interest (e.g. flu-related searches for flu levels). — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

## R

Raster / vector
:   GIS formats: grids of cells or pixels (varying data) / points, lines and polygons (firm borders). — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Real-time shadow
:   Digital activity (e.g. social media chatter) giving an immediate picture of interest, outcry, attention and sentiment. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Roaming
:   The ability of a customer to use their phone wherever they are at any point in time. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

## S

Scale / projection
:   Map-to-Earth distance ratio / method of transferring Earth's curved surface to a flat surface (always distorts). — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

SDGs / MDGs
:   17 Sustainable Development Goals agreed by 193 UN member states, replacing the Millennium Development Goals, which expired at the end of 2015. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Sensor (invasive / non-invasive / wearable)
:   A device that measures a signal and collects data. Invasive sensors are implanted; non-invasive ones are wearable or non-wearable. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Social determination of technology
:   The view that what matters is the social or economic system in which technology is embedded. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Social network analysis (SNA)
:   Collecting and analysing ties between actors (in matrix format), complementing traditional attribute statistics: "with whom individuals are" as well as "who individuals are". — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Sprint
:   One iteration in agile development, after which progress is reviewed and the next sprint is planned. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

Standard
:   An "inescapable passage point" that permits interconnection and data flow. Standards are nested, unevenly distributed, relative to communities of practice, increasingly linked, and embody values. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

STS
:   Science and technology studies: the study of the social, political and cultural factors shaping science and technology. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*

## T

Technological determinism
:   The belief that technology is automatically and inherently good or transformative. Rejected in ICT4D. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

Technology-neutral legislation
:   Laws that target a risk or outcome without favouring a specific technology. — *[Unit 1](unit-1-ict.md#key-terms)*

Technology transfer (macro/meso/micro)
:   Policy and national/regional level / supporting institutions (TTOs, incubators, science parks) / individual actors. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Tele-computing
:   Control of one's system by another person at a different location, e.g. remote software installation and maintenance. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Teleconferencing
:   A telephone meeting between two or more locations through a telecommunications system. It is interactive group communication between three or more people, audio-only or audio-video. Also called telephone, phone or audio conferencing. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Telehealth
:   Using telecommunications and virtual technology to deliver care outside traditional facilities. — *[Unit 6](unit-6-technologies.md#key-terms)*

Tele-presence
:   Technologies that let a person feel present, appear present or have an effect of being present via telerobotics at a place other than their true location. A more advanced form of video conferencing. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Telerobotics
:   Control of a robot from a distance. *(Beyond the notes.)* — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Terminal, link, node
:   The parts of a telecommunication network: terminals are the users' end points, links are the connections, nodes are connection points that pass messages on. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

Thematic analysis
:   Sorting qualitative data (interview and discussion notes) by theme, here by the seven dimensions. — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

Theory of planned behaviour
:   Attitude + subjective norm + perceived behavioural control → behavioural intention. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Theory of technological politics
:   Attention to the momentum of large socio-technical systems, technological imperatives, and the adaptation of human ends to technical means. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

Total gap score
:   The sum of the seven dimension scores (0–70), interpreted with Table 3.1. — *[Unit 3](unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#key-terms)*

TRMI
:   Thomson Reuters MarketPsych Indices: 18,000+ indices distilled daily from over 4 million social media sites and 3 million news articles. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

TTO
:   Technology transfer office. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

## U

Universal sampling (n = N)
:   Capturing all there is instead of a random sample. — *[Unit 4](unit-4-development-informatics.md#key-terms)*

User-centred design (UCD)
:   Optimising a design around how users can, want or need to work, rather than forcing users to adapt. — *[Unit 7](unit-7-standards-theory.md#key-terms)*

## V

Video conferencing
:   Telecommunication technologies that let two or more locations communicate by simultaneous two-way video and audio over computer networks. Also called visual collaboration; a type of groupware. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

VSAT
:   Very Small Aperture Terminal: advanced telecom equipment for receiving digital broadcast. — *[Unit 2](unit-2-applications.md#key-terms)*

## W

Waterfall
:   A sequential methodology: analysis → design → development → QA testing → go live → fix defects. Each stage finishes before the next. Fixed scope in paperwork; change is costly. — *[Unit 5](unit-5-design-methodologies.md#key-terms)*
