## Question 1 — ICT, industry and development (Unit 1)

**1a.** Differentiate between ICT and IT in terms of scope, focus and treatment of telecommunications. Explain **three** challenges ICT creates for organisations, individuals and society. <span class="marks">(7 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    **ICT vs IT (3):**

    | Criterion | IT | ICT |
    |---|---|---|
    | Scope | Narrower | Broader: all components related to computer and digital technologies |
    | Focus | **Managing** technologies related to information (software, hardware, networking) | All technologies enabling communication, data sharing and connectivity, plus their application |
    | Telecommunications | **Excluded** from IT management | **Included** |

    So **IT is a subset of ICT**.

    **Challenges (4, any three explained):**

    - **New opportunities for crime (cybercrime):** stealing money, intellectual property and private information, and attacks on critical infrastructure.
    - **Job displacement** through automation.
    - **Social problems** from reduced face-to-face interaction: trolling, cyberbullying, isolation, loneliness, depression.

**1b.** Compare the four segments of the IT component of the ICT industry in terms of up-front investment, margins and main threats. <span class="marks">(7 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    | Segment | Up-front investment | Margins | Main threat |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | **Hardware** | Large; asset-intensive | Low; compete on price as components commoditise | Established firms; Asian manufacturers with low labour costs; consolidation |
    | **Software** | Relatively few resources (intellectual activity) | Not stated in the courseware | Unauthorised copying, so it depends on **IP law** |
    | **Service providers** | Not stated | **Small** | Exchange-rate and labour-cost changes; no economies of scale against multinationals |
    | **Software-plus-services** | Not stated | **Higher** than pure services | Less affected by scale or labour rates |

    *(About 1.75 per segment. Also note the courseware's point that no country excels in all segments.)*

**1c.** A state government wants to become an "ICT hub". It has many graduates but low digital literacy among older workers, few venture capital firms, unreliable broadband outside the capital, and no clear data-protection rules. Using the **four** factors affecting ICT-driven development, diagnose the state's position and recommend **one** action per factor. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    | Factor (1.5 each) | Diagnosis | Action |
    |---|---|---|
    | **Policies** that impact ICT innovation and investment | No data-protection rules (online security and privacy) | Enact and enforce data-protection and IP rules; technology-neutral legislation |
    | **Human capacity building** | Graduates available, but low digital literacy among older workers | Digital-literacy programmes; ICT skills training |
    | **Enterprise and entrepreneurialism** | Few venture capital firms (access to capital) | Tax incentives for ICT investment; a predictable regulatory framework to attract investors |
    | **Infrastructure and access** | Poor broadband outside the capital | Competition and investment in rural broadband; shared infrastructure |

## Question 2 — ICT in health (Units 2 and 6)

**2a.** Explain the impact of ICT on medical science (Unit 2) and the **three** areas Eric Topol identified as key to the digital transformation of health care. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    **Medical science (3):** medical devices and equipment have evolved considerably through ICT; ICT improves the **acquisition, retrieval, storage and use** of information in health and biomedicine; most medical devices rely on ICT for **use, detection and treatment** of disease; **health informatics tools** include formal medical terms, clinical guidelines, computers, and information and communication systems.

    **Topol's three areas (1 each):** **telemedicine**; **smartphone apps** (the smartphone is the key m-Health tool, used by nearly 80% of adults in high-income countries to access the internet); **sensors and wearables**.

**2b.** Explain how tele-presence differs from video conferencing, and how one of its features (tele-computing) could support a rural clinic. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **Video conferencing (1.5):** simultaneous two-way video and audio over computer networks; visual collaboration; sharing files, applications and whiteboard.
    - **Tele-presence (2.5):** technologies that let a person **feel, appear, or have an effect of being present** elsewhere via **telerobotics** (life-size screens, robots). It is more advanced, with four features: **telecommunication and networking**, **tele-computing**, **messaging**, and **information search, retrieval and archival**.
    - **Tele-computing for a rural clinic (2):** **control of one's system by another person at a different location**. An IT technician in the city can take over the clinic's computer remotely to **install or fix** the EHR software, so the clinic needs no on-site expert and no travel.

**2c.** A federal hospital introduces remote blood-pressure and glucose monitoring for patients with chronic illness. Many older patients drop out; doctors refuse to review readings because they are not paid for it; readings cannot be imported into the hospital EHR.

1. Using the courseware, explain the potential benefit of such self-care. <span class="marks">(3 marks)</span>
2. Identify **two** e-Health barriers in the case and recommend measures. <span class="marks">(5 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    1. **Benefit (3):** self-care based on remotely collected data, as with **Freestyle Libre** glucose sensors in type 1 diabetes, gave **fewer hypoglycaemic episodes** and contact with the health system **only as an exception**. The principle is expected to spread to **blood pressure in hypertension**. Patients make better personal decisions with real-life data and less delay than hospital-only monitoring.
    2. **Barriers (about 2.5 each, any two):**
        - **Awareness, confidence and access:** older age, low health literacy and low socio-economic status reduce uptake. *Measure:* **patient education**, **co-design** with older patients, **financial support** for devices.
        - **Lack of reimbursement** (inadequate legal frameworks): *measure:* reimbursement schemes for remote review; **workflow redesign** so doctors act as diagnosticians and educators.
        - **Lack of interoperability:** readings cannot enter the EHR. *Measure:* interoperability standards and integration, which the courseware says is "becoming ever easier".

## Question 3 — Gaps and participation (Units 3 and 5)

**3a.** Draw and annotate the design–reality gap framework. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    See the [framework and drawing steps](../unit-3-design-reality-gap.md#how-to-draw-the-framework-in-the-exam).

    - **Boxes (1):** System design / Current reality.
    - **Seven ITPOSMO rows on both sides (2):** Information, Technology, Processes, Objectives and values, Staffing and skills, Management systems and structures, Other resources; Outside world as an optional eighth.
    - **Double arrows and Design ↔ Reality "Gap" footer (1).**
    - **Annotations (2):** 0 = no gap, 5 = some change, 10 = complete change; total 0–70 interpreted with the five bands; larger total → greater risk of failure.

**3b.** Explain the **three** types of participatory design and state which you would use when users must "own" the product but a professional team must keep final control. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **User-centred design (1.5):** iterative; users give opinions after each iteration and can make important choices about features; feedback is collected from the earliest stages.
    - **Co-design (1.5):** all participants have **similar rights**; users work with designers and basically **"own the product"**, but **the designer has the final decision**.
    - **User-created design (1.5):** four stages: gather feedback on context → specify requirements → design against them → evaluate against them.
    - **Choice (1.5):** **co-design**, because it combines user ownership with designer control of final decisions.

**3c.** A university's new e-learning platform is six months into implementation and struggling. Lecturers were not consulted; there are only 2 of 6 planned support staff; most lecturers prefer face-to-face teaching; the platform works well technically; internet on campus is adequate.

1. Rate the seven ITPOSMO dimensions with a brief justification, total them and interpret the result. <span class="marks">(5 marks)</span>
2. Explain how participatory design could help close the largest gaps. <span class="marks">(3 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    1. A defensible rating (±1 accepted with justification):

        | Dimension | Score | Reason |
        |---|---|---|
        | Information | 4 | Course content not yet adapted |
        | Technology | 2 | Platform works; internet adequate |
        | Processes | 6 | Teaching routines must change |
        | Objectives & values | 8 | Lecturers prefer face-to-face; not consulted |
        | Staffing & skills | 7 | 2 of 6 support staff; lecturers untrained |
        | Management systems & structures | 5 | Support unit under-staffed |
        | Other resources | 4 | Not stated as a problem |
        | **Total** | **36** | |

        36 → **29–42**: might fail totally or be a partial failure **unless action is taken**. This is a **mid-implementation** analysis, like the courseware's e-government case. *(scores 2, total 1, band 1, mid-implementation 1)*
    2. **PD (3):** the largest gaps are **objectives and values** and **staffing**. **Recruit lecturers** as participants and **define goals** together (mutual learning, respect); use **diverge/converge** sessions to adapt the platform to their teaching (e.g. blended options); **follow up** with working changes. Engagement builds **ownership and belonging**, which changes attitudes, and sessions surface the training and support they need.

## Question 4 — Big data characteristics and challenges (Unit 4)

**4a.** Explain universal sampling and the real-time shadow as characteristics of big data, giving **two** development examples of each. <span class="marks">(8 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    **Universal sampling (4):** big data replaces random sampling with the ambition to **capture all there is (n = N)**. Mobile phones have **over 95%** global penetration and about **75%** access among those on **US$1/day** or less, giving data on society as a whole **without sampling mechanisms**. *Examples:* socio-economic level predicted from call records with **80–85%** accuracy (poverty measurement); **rural Kenya** travel and migration patterns for the spread of **malaria**; Haiti movements after the earthquake and cholera; Rwanda charity and reciprocal aid.

    **Real-time shadow (4):** big data is often **available in real time**; online chatter gives a "real-time digital shadow" of interest, outcry, attention and sentiment. *Examples:* **Estonia** uses phone location data for border-crossing statistics ("biggest advantage … is its speed"); **rain attenuation** between towers measures rainfall for farmers; **Haiti cholera 2010** appeared in tweets **two weeks** before official statistics; 184.5 million US Twitter mentions in 2014.

**4b.** Explain the concept of the digital footprint and why social ties are called the "neglected other half" of social analysis. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **Digital footprint (2):** the data trail created with every digital communication and transaction, produced **almost inevitably**, **as a by-product, without explicit intention**, at **low cost**.
    - **Neglected other half (4):** traditional statistics collected **attributes** of independent agents (income, demographics, location, gadgets), i.e. **"who individuals are"**. Ties were mostly **invisible** because they **grow exponentially** with the number of people and are costly to record. Footprints record ties anyway, enabling **social network analysis** (matrix format) of **"with whom individuals are"**, which is often a **more powerful predictor of behaviour**. SNA concepts (gatekeeper, broker, social capital…) formalise development discussions; network approaches have guided reproductive-health campaigns and microfinance diffusion; the Obama 2012 campaign made SNA **actionable**.

**4c.** A ministry hires a local consultancy to "predict" which LGAs will adopt a new e-agriculture app, using farmers' Facebook data. The consultancy's report shows a high R² but no test on new data. The ministry has no data-protection officer.

Identify **two** challenges from the courseware shown in this case and recommend a response for each. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **Human resource and skills (3):** a high **R²** is **explanatory**, not predictive. Kalampokis et al. found more than a third of social-media studies claiming prediction ran no predictive analytics (Shmueli & Koppius: only 13% in IS research). *Response:* require **out-of-sample testing**; train ministry analysts; collaborate with data-savvy researchers.
    - **Institutional and cultural (3):** **privacy and discrimination** risks (2014 White House report), with weak institutions and no data-protection officer. *Response:* data-protection rules and an officer; anonymisation; **evidence-based processes** and incentives for proper data use.
    - *(Also acceptable: **access**: Facebook users ≠ all farmers, so n ≠ N; fuse with survey data.)*

## Question 5 — Information infrastructures and GIS (Unit 6)

**5a.** Explain **four** properties of information infrastructures and the meaning of "infrastructuring". <span class="marks">(7 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    **Properties (about 1.25 each, any four):** **embeddedness** (sunk into other structures); **transparency** (taken for granted); **reach/scope** (spatial and temporal); **built on an installed base** ("does not grow", must interoperate); **visible upon breakdown**; open to many types of users; interconnected modules; an evolving ecosystem; stretched over decades.

    **Infrastructuring (2):** the **ongoing collective practice** of creating, implementing, using, maintaining and repairing infrastructures, managing their tensions. Design is **continuous, not discrete**; no single actor controls it ("web of infrastructuring"). Only the **user at the terminal** is visible, while assemblage and articulation are invisible. IIs "**occur**" when tensions are resolved.

**5b.** Explain data friction and the **two** responses to it, and **three** characteristics of standards. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **Data friction (1):** imperfect operability at interfaces between groups, organisations and machines, where data is garbled, misinterpreted or lost.
    - **Responses (2):** **metadata development** ("data of data", classes/taxa; no unique scheme; "almost standards"); **standardisation** (project and dataset streams; an unstable process of ordering).
    - **Characteristics (1 each, any three):** nested; unevenly distributed; relative to communities of practice; increasingly linked; codify ethics and values.

**5c.** A state wants to use GIS to decide where to build five new primary health centres. Explain which data layers it should capture (naming sources and formats) and how GIS maps would support the decision. <span class="marks">(7 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **Layers and sources (4):** population density and demographics (**spreadsheet/table** data, census); existing health facilities (**vector points**); roads and rivers (**vector lines**, cartographic data); settlements and land use (**satellite / remote sensing**, **raster**); flood-prone or elevation areas (**raster**). Paper maps must be **scanned**; digital data uploaded. Align **scales** and **projections**.
    - **Decision support (3):** overlay layers with **location as the key**. Show **numbers and density**, as in the courseware's "how many doctors there are in a neighbourhood compared with the area's population"; find under-served, populated areas reachable by road and outside flood zones; click a site for details; update maps easily as new data arrives.

## Question 6 — Standards, technology transfer and engineering (Unit 7)

**6a.** Explain the roles of ISO/IEC 38500, COBIT, ITIL, DAMA-DMBOK and CMMI, and the courseware's advice on adopting them. <span class="marks">(7 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    *(1.2 each)* **ISO/IEC 38500:** starting point; six principles for corporate governance of IT; the board's role; no processes. **COBIT:** generic, process-based governance and management. **ITIL:** best practice for the ICT service lifecycle. **DAMA-DMBOK:** overall data management. **CMMI:** software development.

    **Advice (1):** adoption needs significant administrative effort and culture change, so adopt as **medium-term capacity-building** in **priority areas**; no single standard covers everything.

**6b.** Using **one** study for each, explain the macro, meso and micro perspectives of technology transfer. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    *(2 each)* **Macro:** policy instruments and effectiveness, e.g. Smith et al. (2017): the US Advanced Technology Programme improved long-term **firm survival**. **Meso:** supporting institutions, e.g. van Stijn et al. (2017): 14 university–start-up interaction practices on Route 128; near-market start-ups may be better supported outside the university. **Micro:** individual actors, e.g. Ratzinger et al. (2017): founders' higher education helps digital start-ups reach equity milestones.

**6c.** The University of Ibadan wants more student ICT innovations to become real companies. Using the engineering-mindset features **and** findings on spin-offs, recommend **four** actions and justify each. <span class="marks">(7 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    *(about 1.75 each, any four with justification)*

    - **Context-rich projects** (mindset i): set students real local problems (e.g. cold-chain for vaccines) rather than toy exercises, so innovations have users and markets.
    - **Explicit design process, evaluation and persistence** (ii, vii, viii): teach an EDP with prototype testing and treat failure as learning, which improves product quality.
    - **Teams and dual configuration** (ix + Ben-Hafaïedh): form teams pairing student/academic inventors with **practitioners**, the configuration that performs best commercially.
    - **Flexible, tailored TTO support** (Kolb & Wagner; van Stijn): different spin-off categories need different support. Decide the scope (science-based vs near-market) and partner with external hubs for near-market needs.
    - **University-wide entrepreneurship education** (Ratzinger): entrepreneurship and cross-disciplinary skills in all degrees.
