## Question 1 — ICT and its applications (Units 1–2)

**1a.** Define Information and Communication Technology (ICT). Explain **four** developmental characteristics of ICTs that make them relevant to developing countries. <span class="marks">(8 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    **Definition (2):** ICT is a diverse set of technological tools and resources used to **transmit, store, create, share or exchange information** (UIS, 2009), e.g. computers, the Internet, live and recorded broadcasting, and telephony. There is no universal definition because ICT keeps evolving; it is generally taken to mean **all devices, networking components and applications** that let people and organisations interact in the digital world.

    **Four characteristics (1.5 each):**

    - **Knowledge management** (the *defining feature*): ICTs help people **collect, manage, store, retrieve and distribute knowledge**, levelling the playing field with developed-world firms.
    - **Efficiency:** public administration and firms work more productively at lower cost; SMEs can sell directly to customers and serve distant markets.
    - **Networks:** the Internet gives developing-country users **effectively equivalent access** to information, resources and customers, but infrastructure is costly, so the **appropriate** technology must be chosen.
    - **Less expensive and easier to use:** rapid innovation keeps cutting costs and raising usability.
    - *(Also: simply tools; diverse range; multifunctional and interoperable; multipurpose; intertwined with globalisation.)*

**1b.** Differentiate between teleconferencing, video conferencing and tele-presence in terms of the media transmitted, the sense of presence created, and the additional features offered. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    | Criterion | Teleconferencing | Video conferencing | Tele-presence |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | **Media** (2) | Audio-only **or** audio-video; telephone, computer, TV, radio, telegraph, teletypewriter; PBX or VoIP | **Simultaneous two-way video and audio** over computer networks | Video and audio, **plus telerobotics** |
    | **Sense of presence** (2) | Hearing the group (3+ people, 2+ locations) | **Seeing** the person while listening | Feel present, appear present, or **have an effect** of being present elsewhere |
    | **Additional features** (2) | Not stated | **Visual collaboration / groupware**: sharing files, applications, whiteboard; VoIP | **Four features:** telecommunication and networking; tele-computing; messaging; information search, retrieval and archival |

**1c.** A microfinance bank is opening a branch in a remote LGA with no landline or fibre, weak mobile data, and frequent power cuts. Customers are mainly traders and farmers.

1. Recommend **three** ICT-based gadgets from the courseware for the branch and justify each. <span class="marks">(3 marks)</span>
2. Identify **one** factor affecting ICT-driven development (Unit 1 §1.14) that threatens the branch, and explain how the bank or government could address it. <span class="marks">(3 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    1. *(1 each)* **VSAT:** a very small aperture terminal gives satellite connectivity where there is no landline or fibre. **ATM:** balance enquiry, transfers, deposits and withdrawals without visiting a city branch. **POS / automated cash register:** traders can take card and transfer payments. *(Radio for financial-literacy broadcasts and GSM for SMS alerts are also acceptable with justification.)*
    2. **Infrastructure and access** *(1)*: no fibre, weak data and power cuts limit connectivity. **Response** *(2)*: solar and battery back-up; VSAT; lobby for government/USPF rural infrastructure and **competition** among providers to lower costs. *(Alternative: **human capacity**: customers lack digital literacy, so run training and use simple interfaces.)*

## Question 2 — Design–reality gap (Unit 3)

**2a.** Explain the design–reality gap model and **four** of its ITPOSMO dimensions. <span class="marks">(7 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    **Model (3):** an **ICT4D framework** that records the **gap between a project's design requirements or assumptions and the current reality**. Each gap is scored **0 (no gap) to 10 (radical gap)**, estimated by **researchers, interviewees or group discussion**, along **seven ITPOSMO dimensions**. The **total (0–70)** is interpreted with a score table: the bigger the gaps, the higher the risk of failure.

    **Four dimensions (1 each), e.g.:**

    - **Information:** information required by the design vs information actually used.
    - **Technology:** technology the design requires vs what exists.
    - **Objectives and values:** what stakeholders must want for success vs their real objectives and values.
    - **Management systems and structures:** structures required (e.g. an ICT department) vs actual.

**2b.** Explain why the design–reality gap model is important to e-government managers, and show how it was used as a **mid-implementation** tool in the courseware's e-government case. <span class="marks">(5 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    **Importance (3):** it **focuses management attention** on key issues; it **digs beyond technical issues** to human and organisational factors; it gives a **systematic, credible basis** for reporting and analysis.

    **Mid-implementation use (2):** in a city e-government project, gap analysis found large gaps in **management** (no ICT department, no permanent IT staff), **staffing** (5 of 9 staff, under-qualified), **objectives and values** (corruption) and **information** (missing module, no digitised documents). Discussion with senior managers identified the **small gaps behind partial success** (champions, process re-design before technology, stable information) and an **action plan** (ICT department, more staff, permanent posts), which would enable the missing module and scanning. It helped steer the project **from partial failure towards greater success**.

**2c.** A state ministry rated its planned e-procurement system before implementation:

| Information | Technology | Processes | Objectives & values | Staffing & skills | Management systems & structures | Other resources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 4 | 7 | 9 | 6 | 7 | 4 |

1. Calculate the total gap and interpret it using the courseware's table. <span class="marks">(2 marks)</span>
2. Identify the **largest** gap and explain why it may be the hardest to close. <span class="marks">(3 marks)</span>
3. Propose **three** actions the ministry should take before implementation. <span class="marks">(3 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    1. 5 + 4 + 7 + 9 + 6 + 7 + 4 = **42** → band **29–42**: "the project might fail totally, or might well be a partial failure unless action is taken to close design-reality gaps." It sits at the **top edge**, close to 43–56 ("may well fail"). *(1 + 1)*
    2. **Objectives and values (9).** Key stakeholders' real objectives conflict with the system's, e.g. officials who benefit from opaque contract awards resist transparency. As in the courseware's case, such gaps (corruption) need **"a much longer process of cultural change"**; they cannot be bought like hardware. *(1 + 2)*
    3. *(1 each)* Secure **visible political commitment** and incentives for transparency (address objectives and values and the "lack of commitment" cause of failure). **Re-design procurement processes before** introducing the technology (processes, 7). Create an **ICT unit with permanent, trained staff** (management 7, staffing 6). Then **re-rate** the dimensions before go-live, since this is a **pre-hoc** analysis.

## Question 3 — Development informatics and big data (Unit 4)

**3a.** Explain the two-fold core of big data and its "ultimate limitation", using the Google Flu Trends case. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **New data sources (2):** digital footprints replace surveys with low-cost, real-time **proxy indicators**. Google used **50 million** search terms as a proxy for flu.
    - **Analytics for decisions (2):** whatever the scale, analytic treatment of data is placed at the forefront of **intelligent decision-making**. Google tested **450 million** models and found **45** terms that beat traditional flu models in real time. This is the move from Information Societies to **Knowledge Societies**.
    - **Limitation (2):** **all data is from the past**. As search behaviour changed, the same algorithm drifted out of sync. Analytics can at best **nowcast**; it fails when a system's dynamic changes. This is the **Lucas critique** (economics), **Goodhart's law** (finance) and **Campbell's law** (education), all from 1976.

**3b.** Explain data fusion, contrasting the Human Development Index with the Thomson Reuters MarketPsych Indices. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **Data fusion (2):** combining data from different sources (mobile, social media, network, news) so that **redundancy** compensates for each source being **messy and incomplete**. It is the "assigned remedy" for big data's incompleteness.
    - **HDI (2):** the main development barometer, but **coarse-grained**: only **four indicators** (life expectancy, adult literacy, school enrolment ratio, GDP per capita). Alternatives such as happiness indices need **costly surveys**.
    - **TRMI (2):** distils **daily** over **4 million social media sites** and **3 million news articles**, measuring emotions, opinions and topics, into **18,000+** fine-grained, **real-time** indices (wellbeing, happiness, security, fear, stress, trust, anger). It combines the advantages of the coarse indices through fusion.

**3c.** An agency plans to use Twitter sentiment to decide which states should receive extra funding for youth employment schemes.

1. Explain **two** problems with this plan using the courseware's challenges. <span class="marks">(4 marks)</span>
2. Recommend how the plan could be improved. <span class="marks">(4 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    1. *(2 each)* **Access / representativeness:** "Twitter does not represent 'all people'". Users are urban, educated and connected, so n ≠ N, as in Rwanda, where data came from the privileged; the **bandwidth divide** persists even as access grows. **Lucas critique:** once funding depends on sentiment, behaviour changes (campaigns, bots, coordinated posting), so the indicator stops meaning what it did, just as Flu Trends drifted. *(Also acceptable: **institutional/cultural**: privacy and discrimination; **skills**: misuse of explanatory statistics as "prediction".)*
    2. *(about 1 each)* **Fuse** Twitter data with surveys, labour statistics and mobile data (data fusion); **check representativeness** against census figures; use the data to **complement**, not replace, official statistics; build **theory-driven models** and test policies before allocating funds; apply **privacy safeguards**; train staff in proper **predictive** analytics.

## Question 4 — Design methodologies (Unit 5)

**4a.** Explain the waterfall, agile and lean startup methodologies, and state **one** type of project each is best suited to. <span class="marks">(8 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **Waterfall (about 2.5):** sequential: analysis → design → development → QA testing → go live → fix defects; each stage completes before the next; **fixed scope** in paperwork; formal and bureaucratic; changes are costly (documents and code). *Suits* stable, fully specified requirements (e.g. a legally defined calculation).
    - **Agile (about 2.5):** response to waterfall's drawbacks; **self-organised cross-functional teams**; evolutionary **sprints** reviewed with the customer; less paperwork; "**tames the change**". *Suits* projects with changing requirements.
    - **Lean Startup (about 2.5):** prioritise only **essential** features; build the **MVP** (Minimum Viable Product) first. *Suits* new ventures with limited funds and uncertain demand.
    - *(1 mark spread across appropriate examples.)*

**4b.** Explain **three** principles and **three** downsides of participatory design. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    **Principles (1 each, any three):** focus on the user; mutual learning; empowerment; inclusiveness; stages (agile-style iterations); respect.

    **Downsides (1 each, any three):** **time** (more opinions to process); **sample size** (cannot represent every segment); **structure** (needs a strong collaborative structure or becomes chaotic); **quality** (designers may lack resources to fulfil user desires).

**4c.** A software company sells a school-management system to state ministries of education. Teachers who use it daily complain it is slow and confusing, but ministries keep renewing the contract. Using the courseware, explain the company's risk and recommend how it should use participatory design. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **Situation (2):** the courseware's **K-12 B2B case**: buyers (ministries) ≠ users (teachers). Renewals continue despite poor usability, so there is **no incentive to innovate**, and metrics are **sales only**.
    - **Risk (1):** a **precarious position**: a competitor with better usability can win users' support and **take the customers**.
    - **Recommendation (3):** use **co-design** or **user-centred design** with teachers: **recruit** teachers and administrators from different school types; **define goals** (speed, simplicity); **set up a structure** (milestones, journey map); **diverge/converge** in sessions with facilitators and recording; **follow up** by showing improved builds. Benefits include **innovation**, early error-catching (**cost-effectiveness**), **engagement** and **better inclusion**.

## Question 5 — Technologies (Unit 6)

**5a.** Define e-Health and explain **three** findings about electronic health records (EHRs) reported in the courseware. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    **Definition (2):** (WHO) the use of ICT **in support of health and health-related fields**.

    **EHR findings (any three, about 1.3 each):**

    - **Mixed reception** among US primary care physicians: **63%** said EHRs improved care, but **74%** said they increased workload and **68%** said they took time from patients; **49%** of clinician time went to EHRs and administration.
    - **No unified EHR in Europe**: organisations buy their own software; **interoperability** problems with transfer, coding, searchability and language.
    - **Citizen access:** **90%** of EU citizens expect access to their own data, but access is limited. The **eHDSI** aimed at cross-border EHR and e-prescription sharing in **22 countries** by **2021**.
    - m-Health data must be viewable, importable into the EHR and shareable, with an **audit trail**.

**5b.** Differentiate between G2C, G2B, G2G and G2E e-government, giving **one** example of each. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    *(1.5 each)*

    - **G2C:** government to citizens; the **most common** type; websites, less time and cost, participation and accountability. *E.g. online passport application.*
    - **G2B:** to businesses; regulations, forms, taxes, licences, registration, permits; transparency in contracting. *E.g. online company registration.*
    - **G2G:** between government bodies via a **super government database**; streamlined coordination. *E.g. police–immigration data sharing.*
    - **G2E:** to employees; the **least researched**; leave, salary records, e-learning, benefits. *E.g. staff payslip portal.*

**5c.** A State Emergency Management Agency wants to plan for floods and to stop losing its paper records of past disasters.

1. Explain how GIS could support its flood planning, stating which file format (raster or vector) suits **two** named data layers. <span class="marks">(5 marks)</span>
2. Recommend an archiving approach and explain **two** of its benefits. <span class="marks">(3 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    1. **GIS (3):** overlays layers on one map using location as the key: rivers, elevation, settlements, roads, shelters. It shows **which homes and businesses are in flood-prone areas**, **where to locate emergency shelters**, and **what routes people should take to safety**; satellite data shows change over time. **Formats (2):** **raster** for **elevation / satellite imagery / rainfall** (continuously varying); **vector** for **roads, LGA boundaries, shelter locations** (firm borders or points).
    2. **Digital archiving** with a DMS and archiving software (not paper or scan-on-demand, because paper is vulnerable to the very floods it records). *(1)* Benefits *(1 each)*: **protect documents** against destruction, loss and theft; **secure institutional knowledge** (a central repository of past disasters, retrievable when needed). *(Also: meet retention deadlines; minimise liability risks.)*

## Question 6 — Standards and theoretic concerns (Unit 7)

**6a.** "Technologies are ways of building order in our world." Explain this statement using **two** examples from the courseware. <span class="marks">(6 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **Meaning (2):** devices and systems allow different ways of ordering human activity, and the structures societies choose (consciously or not) shape how people work, communicate, travel and consume for a long time. Technologies can **embody power and authority**, like legislation.
    - **Example 1 (2):** **Robert Moses' ~200 low overpasses** on Long Island kept **12-ft buses**, and so poor and Black transit users, off the parkways. Design settled a social issue.
    - **Example 2 (2):** **McCormick's $500,000 moulding machines** produced inferior castings at higher cost and were abandoned after three years, but they **destroyed the union**. *(Or the **tomato harvester**: about 4,000 → 600 growers and 32,000 jobs lost; a "stacked deck" with no plot.)*

**6b.** Explain **five** challenges that INGOs face in implementing ICT for development. <span class="marks">(5 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    *(1 each)* **Sustainability and scale** (ad hoc pilots, few at scale); **lack of knowledge** (no expertise or capacity; ICT seen as a back-office issue with a poor reputation); **pace of change** (momentum favours incremental change over new ways of working); **funding** (cyclical donor funding, pressure on admin costs); **changing roles and norms** (ICT narrows the gaps in understanding, information, access, communication and resources that INGOs were founded to bridge).

**6c.** A telecom company and an NGO plan an SMS service to give nutrition advice to mothers in rural communities. Earlier ICT kiosks in the area failed because their content was "too general" and nobody evaluated them. Using the courseware's user-centred design framework, explain how you would design and evaluate the service, including the role of the theory of planned behaviour. <span class="marks">(9 marks)</span>

??? success "Model answer"
    - **Why the old kiosks failed (1):** the courseware's fronts: **irrelevant, general content**, **fragmented single-discipline development**, and **no evaluation**.
    - **Exploratory research + TPB (3):** identify target mothers, needs, norms; **baseline survey** of nutrition knowledge; design brief; four contextual factors (socio-cultural, technological, economic, past ICT use). A TPB questionnaire covers **attitude** (do mothers value SMS advice?), **subjective norm** (do husbands, mothers-in-law and religious leaders approve?) and **perceived behavioural control** (phone access, literacy, ability to change diets).
    - **Creative design (2):** build and iterate a **Knowledge Aggregation Platform** (e.g. SMS plus voice in the local language) with mothers; apply **persuasive technology** cues (trusted local voices, timely reminders) to shape norms and raise perceived control; collect feedback on **Rogers' five attributes**.
    - **Evaluative research (2):** a **longitudinal** study of a cohort, comparing **baseline vs intervention** (knowledge, feeding practices, use), and evaluation on the five attributes to explain adoption.
    - **Multidisciplinary team (1):** social science (norms), computer science (system), industrial design (interface, usability), marketing (sustainability, adoption).
