# Exam technique

How to turn what you know into marks in the CSC 584 essay paper.

!!! tip "Short on time?"
    The [crash course](crash-course.md) compresses the whole course onto one page: frameworks, traps, confusable pairs, answer outlines and flashcards.

## The paper

| Feature | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Time | **2 hours** |
| Instruction | **Attempt any THREE (3) questions** (usually from five or six) |
| Each question | Parts **a, b, c** (sometimes sub-parts i, ii, iii), marks shown in brackets, about **20 marks** in total |
| Part (c) | Usually a **scenario**: a short real-world case where you must identify, classify, recommend and **justify** |

### Time plan

- **3 questions in 120 minutes = 40 minutes each.** Spend about **5 minutes** at the start choosing questions, and keep **5 minutes** at the end to check.
- That leaves about **35 minutes per question**, or **under 2 minutes per mark**. A 2-mark part needs a few sentences, not a page.
- **Choose by part (c), not part (a).** Everyone can define terms. The scenario decides your grade, so pick the questions whose scenarios you can handle.
- Start with your **strongest** question to bank marks and confidence.

## Command words: what each one wants

| Command word | What the examiner wants | Typical marks |
|---|---|---|
| **Define / describe briefly** | A precise definition, ideally the courseware's wording, plus one clarifying sentence | 1–2 per term |
| **Mention / list / identify / enumerate** | Names. Add **a few words of explanation** anyway, since it costs little and protects you if the marker expects more | ½–1 each |
| **Explain** | What it is + **how or why** it works or matters | 1–2 per point |
| **Discuss** | Explain, **then** consider importance, implications, examples or limitations | 2–3 per point |
| **Differentiate / distinguish / compare** | Compare **on named criteria**. A **table** is ideal. If criteria are given ("in terms of…"), use exactly those | 1–2 per criterion |
| **Account for** | Name each item **and explain** it (Unit 2: "account for 6 ICT-based gadgets") | 1 per item |
| **Isolate** | Pick out and state the items (Unit 1: "isolate the application areas") | ½–1 each |
| **Recommend and justify** | A clear choice **plus reasons tied to the scenario's facts**, and ideally **why not** the alternatives | 1 choice + 1–2 reasons |
| **Draw / draw and label** | A neat, labelled diagram **plus** a sentence or two explaining it | 2–3 for the diagram |

!!! tip "The \"explain\" rule"
    If a question gives 2 marks per point, write **two ideas per point**: a statement and its explanation or example. One bare phrase earns one mark at best.

## How to write an answer that earns marks

Model answers on this site follow this pattern, and so should yours:

1. **Open with a definition** (one or two sentences) using the courseware's key words.
2. **One point per paragraph**, with a **bold heading**: e.g. "**Sample size.** It is nearly impossible to involve a representative of each audience segment, so…"
3. **Use the courseware's numbers and names.** "ICT changed medical science" is weak. "Health informatics tools include formal medical terms, clinical guidelines, computers and information and communication systems" earns the mark.
4. **Give an example**, especially a Nigerian one, when the question says "discuss" or "with examples".
5. **Match the count.** If asked for four, give four clearly numbered points. A fifth rarely earns extra marks but costs time.
6. **Finish part (c) with a decision.** "Therefore I recommend agile, because…"

### Answering scenario questions

Use **I-A-J-A**:

| Step | Do this |
|---|---|
| **I**dentify | Name the concept the scenario is testing (e.g. "This is a **sample representativeness (n vs N)** problem.") |
| **A**pply | Point to the **facts in the scenario** that show it ("penetration is only 35% and phones are owned by traders…") |
| **J**ustify | Link to the courseware's evidence or case ("as in Rwanda 2005–09, where phones were owned by the privileged strata") |
| **A**ct | Recommend a measure or decision, and **why it beats the alternatives** |

!!! example "Weak vs strong"
    **Weak:** "The project will fail because of poor staff."

    **Strong:** "Staffing and skills is the largest gap (8/10): only 3 IT officers serve 50 centres and no clinic staff are trained. As in the courseware's e-government case, the state should first create permanent IT posts and train staff, because the missing technical components cannot be maintained without them."

## When the courseware's numbers don't match

The courseware sometimes asks for a different number of items than it lists (see the "Courseware note" boxes in each unit). Rule:

> **Give what the question asks for, from what the courseware actually lists, and say so briefly.**

| Question asks for | Courseware lists | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 8 dimensions of the DRG model | 7 ITPOSMO + Outside world in Figure 3.2 | Give all 8 (Unit 3) |
| 3 areas where video conference differs from tele-presence | 4 features | Give 3 (or all 4) of the features (Unit 2) |
| 6 INGO challenges | 5 | Give 5 and note "the courseware identifies five" (Unit 7) |
| 6 stern advice | 5 | Give 5 (Unit 7) |
| 10 standardisation organisations | 9 + ISO/IEC 38500 / ISSA | Count ISO/IEC 38500 or ISSA to reach 10 (Unit 7) |
| 5 areas where ICT can be applied | 3 in §2.1 | Add §2.3 sectors (Unit 2) |
| 3 opportunities for development | Not listed | Use the three in the Unit 4 §4.6.1 Exam-angle box |

## Common mistakes

1. **Listing instead of explaining.** "Explain four…" with four one-word bullets earns about a quarter of the marks.
2. **Ignoring the scenario's facts.** A recommendation that would suit any case earns little. Quote the case.
3. **Mixing up neighbouring concepts:**
    - video conferencing vs tele-presence;
    - G2B vs G2G;
    - raster vs vector;
    - Lucas critique vs Goodhart's law (same idea, different field);
    - two-fold core of big data vs goals of ICT4D;
    - co-design vs user-centred design;
    - colonisation vs colonialism;
    - social determination vs technological determinism.
4. **Arithmetic slips in DRG questions.** Show the sum, check the band edges (14, 28, 42, 56, 70), and quote the band's wording.
5. **Running out of time on part (a).** Part (c) is usually worth as much and is harder. Leave it enough time.
6. **Writing your own views as if they were the courseware's.** Present extra knowledge as an example or "beyond the notes" point, after the courseware's content.
